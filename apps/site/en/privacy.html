Privacy

There are two GymYars and they are not alike. The Android app has no account and no server: it collects nothing and sends nothing. The web service has accounts, because coaching needs two people to see the same training — and it holds what you put into it and nothing else.

This page describes both. Where they differ it says so, because a single policy that blurred them would be wrong about one of them.

The Android app

It collects nothing and transmits nothing. There is no sign-in, no sync, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, no tracking of any kind. Your training lives in a file in the app's private storage on your phone and is never sent anywhere.

This is a property of the build rather than a promise: the code that talks to a server throws immediately in the app build, so a future screen that forgot would fail loudly in development instead of quietly calling somewhere. The app makes exactly one kind of outbound request — fetching exercise illustrations from a public CDN — and that request carries no identifier of any kind.

Uninstalling the app removes everything. You can export it all first, through the phone's own share sheet.

The two things the app reads from your phone

Both are local reads. Neither sends anything anywhere, because in the app build there is nowhere to send it.

  • A heart-rate strap, over Bluetooth. Only while you are training, only if you connect one. The reading goes into that session on that phone. Permission to scan is declared as never being used to determine location, which is why the app does not ask for location at all on Android 12 and later.
  • Sessions from Health Connect. If you allow it, the app reads workout sessions and the heart rate recorded during them from Health Connect — the store your watch already writes to. It asks for those two things and nothing else. It does not ask for your route, which is a map of where you run.

The web service

An account exists so that your training is on more than one device and so that a coach you choose can see it. What it holds:

  • Who you are: your name, and an email address or phone number to sign in with. A password is stored only as a hash. Nothing else is asked for — no date of birth, no address, no identity document.
  • Your training: routines, sessions, every set, body weight, weekly check-ins and habits.
  • Health data: heart rate — the average, low and high of a session, the peak around a set, and a daily resting figure. Body weight is health data too. Where this comes from is described below.
  • What you upload: form-check video, progress photos and voice notes, if you record any.
  • Coaching: who is coaching whom, what you have chosen to share with them, the programmes they propose and the messages between you.
  • Payment, if you pay: the amount, the date and the reference the payment provider returns. Card details never reach this service — they are entered on the provider's own page.

Health data, specifically

Heart rate and body weight are treated as more sensitive than the rest, and it is worth being exact about how they arrive:

  • From a file you choose to import — an Apple Health export, or an export from another training app. Nothing is read from that file except training, weight and heart rate, and the file itself is never uploaded: it is read in your browser.
  • From a strap you connect, during a session.
  • From Health Connect, on Android, if you allow it.
  • From an iPhone shortcut you set up yourself, which sends a session to your account as it ends. You create the key it uses, you can see when it was last used, and you can revoke it at any time — from Settings, immediately, without our involvement.

None of it is used for anything but showing it back to you and to a coach you have shared it with. It is not analysed for any other purpose, not aggregated with anyone else's, not sold, and not shared with anybody — no advertiser, no data broker, no insurer, no analytics provider. There is no such relationship to disclose because there is no such relationship.

What a coach can see

Only what you grant, and each kind separately: programmes, sessions, body weight, check-ins, habits, form-check video and progress photos are individual permissions. Heart rate travels with the sessions it belongs to. You can withdraw any of them at any time, and a coach who has lost a permission stops being able to see that data immediately.

A coach cannot edit your training. What they send is a proposal, and it changes nothing until you accept it.

Getting it out, and getting rid of it

  • Export. Everything you have, as a file, at any time, from Settings.
  • Delete. Deleting your account removes your training, your uploads and your account rows. Uninstalling the app does the same for the app build, where there was never a copy anywhere else.

If somebody else is running this

GymYar is open source and anybody may run their own instance. If you signed up on somebody else's server, they hold your data, not us — this page describes what the software does, and the operator of that instance is who to ask about how they run it, where it is hosted and how long they keep backups.

If you are that operator: the software stores what is listed above and makes no outbound request to us or to anyone else on your users' behalf. What you do with the database is your responsibility, and so is the policy you show your users.
