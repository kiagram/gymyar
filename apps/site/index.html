GymYar

Strength in motion, together in growth.

An open-source coaching platform. Coaches program for their clients; clients keep a training log that stays theirs. Run the whole thing on your own server.

Open the app Get GymYar How coaching works

Coach and client

A coach can propose. Only the client can accept.

Most coaching apps make your training log an account your coach administers. GymYar inverts that: the client owns every row, and a coach's work arrives as a proposal that does nothing at all until it is accepted.

📋

Programmes as proposals

A coach drafts a week and sends it. It waits until the client says yes — and only then is it written, as the client's own data, through the same sync path their phone uses.

🔒

Scopes, section by section

Sharing a programme does not share what you weigh. Every coach-side read is gated on what that client actually granted, and it can be narrowed again at any time.

📊

A roster that shows drift

Adherence per client, who has gone quiet, whose loads have stalled — the questions a coach actually opens the app to answer, on one screen.

💬

Messaging in context

Attached to the client and the week it is about, rather than a separate inbox where nobody remembers which session was being discussed.

🧠

Review that reads the log

Stalls, missed sessions and volume drifting away from plan, found in the data and written up as a note a person can read, in the language they use.

✍️

Nothing is applied for you

A generated programme comes back to be looked at. A drafted change fills in the composer the coach already uses. There is no endpoint anywhere that writes someone's training.

The numbers are not guessed. Sets, reps, loads, progression and exercise choice are computed against the real exercise library and the same progression rules the app already runs on. A language model is asked to do two things — turn free text into a structured brief, and write the note explaining a change — and both have a deterministic implementation underneath. With no model configured at all, GymYar builds the same plans and finds the same stalls; it phrases them from a template instead. Nothing can invent a lift that is not in the library, or put 140 kg on a beginner's bar.

Everything a training log needs

Built in the gym, not in a boardroom

GymYar is built on openGym, whose tracker was sharpened on the gym floor long before any of the coaching existed. All of it is still here, and none of it is behind a paywall.

▶️

Guided workouts

Knows what day it is, starts today's session, pre-fills your weights from last time, times your rest, spots PRs.

🏋️

1,324 exercises

Searchable library with animated demos, filterable by body part and the equipment you actually own — plus your own custom exercises.

⌨️

Log a session by typing it

"Squats 5×5 at 100, last set felt like two left." It comes back parsed into sets for you to confirm — never written straight in.

🗓️

Weekly plan

A routine per weekday, and any day can be rescheduled when life happens — without touching the plan.

📈

Progression that follows a rule

Linear, Greyskull LP, double progression or added time — per routine, overridable per exercise. Your weights are already right when the session opens, and every target says why.

⚖️

Body weight and goal

Interactive chart with your goal line; every change coloured by whether it moves toward it.

💪

Muscle map

A body diagram shaded by how much work each muscle got — and which ones got nothing.

🟩

Activity heatmap

A year view of your training time. Streaks included.

🎯

Effort per set

Rate a set as RIR or RPE — whichever scale you already think in. Optional, every set keeps the scale it was logged with, and Stats reads it back.

🤸

Bodyweight training, properly

Push-ups and pull-ups know they have no weight to type. Reps and sets are the progression, and a dip belt reads as an addition.

⏱️

Timed sets, supersets, cardio

Planks and carries logged by time. Pairs run back-to-back on one rest. Cardio logged as time and speed, where weight times reps means nothing.

🧮

Estimated 1RM

Per exercise, from your best eligible set — and it names which one. Will not guess above 12 reps.

🌍

12 languages, including Persian

Full UI translation with a properly mirrored RTL layout, exercise instructions in 10 languages, and weeks that start on the day your locale starts them.

📥

Bring your history

Import from FitNotes, Strong, Hevy — including the RPE they recorded — or body weight straight out of an Apple Health export.

📦

Yours to keep

One-tap JSON export and import. Your training log is a file you own, not a row in someone's cloud.

Pricing

Training is free. It stays free.

Logging, programmes, history, stats — everything above under "features" — is ungated and always will be. A subscription exists on the coach side of a hosted instance, and it buys three things.

🆓 Training

For everyone. No tier, no trial, no card.

  • Every tracker feature, forever
  • Sync across your own devices
  • Reading a roster you already built
  • Accepting and running a coach's programme
  • A client is never gated at all

A coach whose payment lapses cannot take their clients' training away. It was never the coach's to take.

💼 Coaching

For coaches, on a hosted instance that charges.

  • Taking on new clients
  • Proposing programmes to them
  • Messaging them
  • Nothing else sits behind it

The paid tier is a property of a deployment, not of the software. Self-host with no payment provider configured and coaching is simply free — which is also the only honest reading of the licence. Hosting can be charged for; the code cannot.

Why GymYar exists

openGym solved one person tracking their own training, and solved it well. It could not become two people — a coach and a client — without changing how the data was stored, because a whole-account overwrite silently destroys one of two simultaneous edits. That rewrite is GymYar.

This instance, right now

Counted in the database, not written into the copy

Every number below is read from the same Postgres the app writes to, and recomputed a few times an hour. It describes the instance you are looking at — not the project's total, and not a figure anyone typed into this page.

—people training
—sessions logged
—working sets
—tonnes lifted
—coaches

Counts and one sum, and nothing else. The endpoint behind them takes no account, reads no row belonging to anybody, and cannot be asked about a person. Warm-up sets and unfinished sessions are left out — they are rows, not training. An instance can turn the whole thing off with PUBLIC_STATS=off, and this section then does not appear at all.

Open source

AGPL-licensed, forever

GymYar is a derivative work of openGym and stays on its licence. Every fork stays open, every hosted instance owes its source to the people using it, and there is no closed commercial version waiting behind a curtain.
